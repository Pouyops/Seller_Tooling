## Analysis

Hand-written. The tables above are machine-generated by `make bench`; this section is the reading of them.
Run: Kaggle Tesla T4 (14.6 GB, Linux), 200 synthetic images, commit `8ba45a6`.

### Recommendation: two tiers, not one model

| Tier | Model | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Free tier / bulk** | `birefnet_lite` **fp16** | 7.8 img/s, 0.85 GB VRAM, Q = 0.811. Five times cheaper per image than the quality leader for 0.054 Q less. |
| **Paid tier / hard categories** | `inspyrenet_base` (fp32) | Q = 0.865, best thin-structure recall (0.818) and the only model above 0.5 on loose saffron threads. 1.5 img/s. |
| Watch | `ben2_base` | Q = 0.844 at 1.8 img/s — better carpet-fringe boundary (BF@3px 0.72 vs 0.67) than InSPyReNet and faster. The sensible default if we end up wanting a single model. |

The queue already carries a per-job `model` and `priority`, so tiering costs nothing architecturally:
free users get `birefnet_lite`, paying users get `inspyrenet_base` on the `high` priority stream.

### What the numbers actually say

**1. fp16 is free money on tensor-core GPUs — and must stay off on GTX 16xx.**
`birefnet` fp16 vs fp32 scores identically (Q 0.834 both; IoU 0.940 both) and runs **2.8× faster**
(371 ms vs 1045 ms); `birefnet_lite` is 2.6× faster at identical Q. On the GTX 1650 fp16 produces
all-NaN mattes, which is why the loader probes and falls back (ADR-008). Ship fp16, keep the probe.

**2. Batching does not help, and costs a lot of VRAM.**
Throughput is flat-to-falling with batch size (`birefnet_lite`: 7.77 img/s at bs=1 → 6.07 at bs=16)
while peak VRAM grows 14×. The bottleneck is our own CPU-side preprocessing: `MattingModel.preprocess`
resizes images one at a time on the CPU, and Kaggle gives 4 vCPUs, so a bigger batch just means more
serial CPU work before the GPU starts. **Action:** set `ST_MAX_BATCH=1` for now, and before re-testing
batching, move the resize to the GPU or run it in a thread pool. Don't conclude "batching is useless" —
conclude "our preprocessing is the bottleneck".

**3. Gold jewelry is the worst category for every model** (IoU 0.72–0.84, against 0.94–0.98 elsewhere).
Thin chains are mostly boundary, so IoU punishes a one-pixel error much harder than on a carpet.
Boundary F on the same images is 0.93–0.98, so the models *are* finding the chains. For jewelry,
IoU is the wrong headline metric; BF@3px is the one to watch.

**4. Loose saffron threads are the real failure.** Thin recall on `saffron_packaged`:
`birefnet` 0.02, `birefnet_lite` 0.16, `ben2_base` 0.45, `inspyrenet_base` 0.52. Detached small parts
get dropped by everything. If "saffron with threads scattered around the pack" matters commercially,
no off-the-shelf model solves it — that's a fine-tuning target (H-007 option B).

**5. Two models are unusable as configured.**
- `birefnet_dynamic` collapses on glassware (IoU 0.64 vs 0.95 for plain `birefnet`). It was trained
  for variable resolution and we ran it at a fixed 1024; transparency is where that shows.
- `birefnet_hr` at 2048 is *better* on carpet fringe than anything else (BF@3px 0.75, thin recall 0.97)
  and *far worse* on glass (IoU 0.42). It also costs 5.8 GB and 1.4 s/image. Interesting as a
  per-category specialist later, not as a default.

**6. InSPyReNet runs fp32 only in our adapter.** Given finding 1, testing it in fp16 is the highest-value
cheap experiment left: if it holds quality, the quality leader also becomes ~2× cheaper and the
two-tier split might collapse into one model.

### What these numbers do not establish

- **The benchmark is synthetic** (`docs/dataset-gap.md`). Rankings on procedurally rendered fringe and
  glass may not survive real photographs. Treat gaps under ~0.03 Q as noise.
- **A T4 is not the target card.** 16 GB Turing, not 24 GB Ampere/Ada. Relative speeds should hold;
  absolute throughput and the OOM thresholds will not. H-001 stays open.
- **VRAM "device" column reads 0** on Kaggle — the NVML sampler didn't produce a delta there. The
  PyTorch allocator peaks are the real numbers.
- `birefnet_lite`'s 32 s load time is first-run disk warm-up, not a property of the model.

### Precision experiments (done — `eval/runs/kaggle-fp16`, `eval/runs/kaggle-amp`)

The question was whether the quality leader could get ~2× cheaper the way BiRefNet did. **It can't.**

| model | `.half()` (fp16) | autocast (`amp`) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `birefnet_lite` | **7.77 img/s**, IoU 0.909 | 5.05 img/s, IoU 0.909 | use **fp16**; autocast is 35% slower for identical output |
| `inspyrenet_base` | **fails**: `expected scalar type Float but found Half` | **non-finite outputs**, auto-fell back to fp32 | fp32 only |
| `inspyrenet_fast` | same failure | same | fp32 only |
| `ben2_base` | (upstream autocast) 1.81 img/s | — | amp is worth 1.7× vs its own fp32 (1.06 img/s), same IoU 0.916 |

InSPyReNet builds fp32 tensors inside its pyramid ops, so casting weights breaks the forward pass
outright; under autocast it runs but emits NaN. Both were caught automatically — the `.half()` case by
the loader, the autocast case by the numeric probe (ADR-008) on a GPU where fp16 is otherwise perfectly
fine. That probe has now paid for itself twice on two different failure modes.

**Consequence:** the two-tier recommendation stands unchanged. `birefnet_lite` fp16 for the free tier
(7.8 img/s), `inspyrenet_base` fp32 for paid (1.5–1.8 img/s). Making the quality tier cheaper needs a
different model or patched upstream code, not a precision flag.

**Measurement noise:** the same `inspyrenet_base` fp32 configuration measured 1.54 img/s in two runs and
1.80 in a third on Kaggle's shared hardware — about ±17%. Treat throughput differences smaller than that
as noise unless they reproduce.

### Next experiments, in order

1. GPU-side preprocessing, then re-measure batching honestly.
2. Per-category routing: `birefnet_hr` for carpets only, if fringe quality turns out to sell.
3. If the quality tier needs to be cheaper: try `ben2_base` (amp, 1.81 img/s, best BF@3px of the
   three) as the paid-tier model instead of `inspyrenet_base`, trading thin-structure recall
   (0.708 vs 0.818) for ~20% more throughput.
