## 3. Cost per image

Model `birefnet_lite`. **Sequential** is what the code does today (encode after inference); **pipelined** is the same hardware if CPU encoding overlapped GPU inference — an engineering change, not a purchase.

| hardware | scaling | img/s | effective img/hour | compute | storage | egress | **total/image** | blended¹ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| owned T4 (measured) | 1x | 7.8 | 3,460 | 3.7 | 1.6 | 0.7 | **6.0** | 5.7 |
| rented T4 (measured) | 1x | 7.8 | 3,460 | 13.7 | 1.6 | 0.7 | **16.0** | 14.6 |
| owned 4090 @ 2x T4 | 2x | 15.5 | 4,203 | 3.1 | 1.6 | 0.7 | **5.4** | 5.1 |
| rented 4090 @ 2x T4 | 2x | 15.5 | 4,203 | 11.3 | 1.6 | 0.7 | **13.6** | 12.5 |
| owned 4090 @ 3x T4 | 3x | 23.3 | 4,527 | 2.9 | 1.6 | 0.7 | **5.2** | 4.9 |
| rented 4090 @ 3x T4 | 3x | 23.3 | 4,527 | 10.5 | 1.6 | 0.7 | **12.8** | 11.7 |
| owned 4090 @ 2x, **pipelined** | 2x | 15.5 | 5,351 | 2.4 | 1.6 | 0.7 | **4.7** | 4.5 |

¹ blended = total with a 10% share of byte-identical repeats served from cache (storage + egress only).

**Headline: about 5.1 toman per image** on an owned 4090 at 35% utilisation — assuming the 4090 is twice a T4, which nobody has measured yet (H-001).

## 4. What actually drives the number

| change | new cost/image | vs headline |
|---|---|---|
| utilisation 35% → 70% | 3.7 | -27% |
| utilisation 35% → 15% | 8.8 | +73% |
| toman/USD +50% | 7.5 | +47% |
| electricity ×4 | 5.9 | +17% |
| GPU price −30% | 4.5 | -11% |
| storage ×3, 90-day retention | 18.2 | +259% |
| use `inspyrenet_base` (best quality, 1.5 img/s) | 7.5 | +47% |

Utilisation and the exchange rate dominate. Electricity barely registers at Iranian tariffs; picking the slowest, highest-quality model costs more than any infrastructure decision here.

## 5. Break-even against monthly price points

At 5.1 toman/image, infrastructure is almost invisible against any of these prices. The table below is the brief's break-even, and it mostly proves that compute is not what decides this business.

| price (toman/month) | images before compute eats the fee | infra cost at 100 img | at 500 img | gross margin at 500 img |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50,000 | **9,852** | 508 | 2,538 | 94.9% |
| 100,000 | **19,704** | 508 | 2,538 | 97.5% |
| 150,000 | **29,556** | 508 | 2,538 | 98.3% |
| 200,000 | **39,408** | 508 | 2,538 | 98.7% |
| 300,000 | **59,111** | 508 | 2,538 | 99.2% |
| 500,000 | **98,519** | 508 | 2,538 | 99.5% |

**Free tier:** 20 images/month ≈ **102 toman per active free user**; 1,000 of them ≈ 101,503 toman/month — covered by a single seller paying 150k. Abuse, not cost, is the reason to keep a quota.

### The question that actually matters

Since compute is noise, the real break-even is against **fixed costs** — salaries, support, an office. This model does not know your fixed costs, so pick a row:

**Paying sellers needed to break even** (each using 200 images/month):

| fixed costs (toman/month) | @ 50,000 | @ 100,000 | @ 150,000 | @ 200,000 | @ 300,000 | @ 500,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100,000,000 | 2,041 | 1,010 | 671 | 503 | 334 | 200 |
| 300,000,000 | 6,124 | 3,031 | 2,014 | 1,508 | 1,003 | 601 |
| 600,000,000 | 12,249 | 6,062 | 4,027 | 3,015 | 2,007 | 1,202 |
| 1,000,000,000 | 20,414 | 10,103 | 6,712 | 5,026 | 3,345 | 2,004 |

Read it as: at 150k/month per seller, every 100M toman of monthly fixed cost needs roughly 671 paying sellers. Choose the price from what Iranian sellers will pay and what the competition charges (H-011) — not from this cost floor, which any price above ~2,000 toman/month clears.

## 6. Honest limits of this model

- **Infrastructure only.** No salaries, no support, no payment-gateway fees, no marketing, no tax. At this scale a single developer-month dwarfs every number above; this model tells you the *floor*, not the price.
- **The target GPU was never measured.** Everything is the T4 scaled by a factor (H-001).
- **Every price is a guess** (§2). Exchange rate and utilisation move the answer more than any technical choice, and both are outside engineering control.
- **Quality has a price.** `inspyrenet_base` scores highest but is ~5× the compute of `birefnet_lite`; the two-tier plan in `eval/analysis.md` exists for exactly this reason.
- **Benchmark set is synthetic** (`docs/dataset-gap.md`), so the throughput numbers assume the image sizes it generates (~1 MP JPEGs). Bigger seller photos cost more.
